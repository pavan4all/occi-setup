Partition Setup

  • When should this be done:   Day -2.  Make sure you review all the materials first before start assembling. The new numbering system in the Youtube video will help position the pieces at the right spot and that will make it easier.

  • How many people needed:  6 approx.  Usually we have limited tools, so more people won't help because of limited tools

  • Tools and materials needed:  Toolbox, wrench, ladder, instructions

  • Estimated time: 1 - 1.5 days

Partition Numbering System

Review the Partition Numbering system to get a sense of which piece goes where.  This should help in positioning the right partition with the proper orientation.

There are 5-ft, 3-ft and 2-ft partitions. 

Some partitions are special:

  • 2 partitions that have L-shaped brackets (at top and bottom).  Each of these is an end partition of the back wall and is used to begin building the side walls.

  • 2 partitions that have T-shaped brackets (at top and bottom). These are both used in the back wall to hold the perpendicular, stabilizing 3-foot partitions.

  • 1 partition that is somewhat bent at the bottom edge and has a bracket sticking upwards.  This one is used to build the wall against the men’s shoe rack.


The general rule to connect partitions is  after any 3 partitions are joined together, a stabilizing partition (on the back wall) or T-foot (on the side wall) is required. This stabilizer will be perpendicular to the joined partitions.

The Bottom of a Partition

The bottom edge of each partition has the following:

  1. A bracket screwed on that sticks out a bit.  This bracket will snap onto the nubs of the adjoining partition.  There is also an adjustable round leg (or foot) that can be screwed up or down to set the partition properly on the floor.

  2. Two silver colored nubs on the far side from the bracket. These will snap onto the adjoining partition’s bracket. This side also has the adjustable round leg.


The Top of a Partition

The top edge of each partition has a bracket sticking out that aligns with the holes of the adjoining partition to bolt on using Allen bolts (or screws).

Tools

You should expect to see the follow items in the toolbox

Step 1- Build the Back Wall

The back wall lines up with the cable on the ceiling which hangs a long curtain and it is about 45’ long.  

This wall is built, from left to right, as:

  1. Three 5-foot partitions (with the first one with an L-bracket and the last one with a T-bracket)

  2. One stabilizing 3-foot T-partition

  3. Three 5-foot partitions 

  4. One stabilizing 3-foot T-partition

  5. Three 5-foot partitions (with the last one with an L-bracket)


Tips: Begin with one of the two T-partitions, and expand to the left and & / or right

Step 2 - Build the side wall on the women side

This side wall is to be built about 3’ from the hall’s hard wall.  It consists of four 5-ft partitions and one end 2-ft partition. A T-foot is added to the third 5-ft partition and the end partition (see below on instructions to add a T-foot).

The final 2-ft partition blocks the breeze and noise coming in when the entrance door is opened. See below

Step 3 - Build the side wall on the men side

(This can be done in parallel to the women side)

This side partition wall is to be built about 4.5’ away from the hall’s hard wall (A wide space is kept here because the space is used by male students lining up for their meals as well as for servers to go back and forth). 

It consists of 

  • four 5-ft partitions 

  • one 3-ft partition.  

Add a T-foot after the 3rd partition and after the final end partition.  (See below for instructions on adding a T-foot).

Here is the final view of the side wall on the men side, from the kitchen/dinning hall side

Step 4 Build the wall against the male shoe-rack

  1. On the right, there is a slightly broken partition that has a bracket sticking out and up. This bracket is screwed onto the hard wall at the top. The bottom right of the partition is left unconnected to anything, just resting on the floor.  

2. The left side of the second partition has a T-foot. The other end of this partition is connected in the usual way to the adjoining partition. No round legs are used in either partition.

3. Two additional 2’ partitions are further extended on the left, in an L shape. Here is how it looks like:

Appendix

Connecting partitions with T-foot

A T-foot is added to provide stability to a wall of partitions and is used on the side walls, to replace a T-partition, so as not to block the AT’s view of the students (and the students’ view of the AT). 

A T-foot replaces a bracket at the bottom of the partition. The round adjusting leg is also removed from the end of the partition that has the T-foot. As the bracket is removed, the adjoining partition’s nubs cannot snap into anything. So, to stabilize the connection, the adjoining partition also must have a T-foot and the two T-feet are held together with a long plastic tie.  If the plastic tie is not long enough, connect two of them back-to-back. Wrap the tie around both T-feet and then securely tighten the tie. 

The top of the partitions are bolted on with Allen bolts just like any other. 

For futher stability, tape them together onto the floor as below, with duck tape
