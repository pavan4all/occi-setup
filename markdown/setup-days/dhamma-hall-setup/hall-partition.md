Partition Numbering System

Review the Partition Numbering system to get a sense of which piece goes where.  This should help in positioning the right partition with the proper orientation.

[YouTube Video, Partition Numbering System](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KE86ERbx4cs)

There are 5-ft, 3-ft and 2-ft partitions. 

Some partitions are special:

- 2 partitions that have L-shaped brackets (at top and bottom).  Each of these is an end partition of the back wall and is used to begin building the side walls.
- 2 partitions that have T-shaped brackets (at top and bottom). These are both used in the back wall to hold the perpendicular, stabilizing 3-foot partitions.
- 1 partition that is somewhat bent at the bottom edge and has a bracket sticking upwards.  This one is used to build the wall against the men’s shoe rack.

The general rule to connect partitions is  after any 3 partitions are joined together, a stabilizing partition (on the back wall) or T-foot (on the side wall) is required. This stabilizer will be perpendicular to the joined partitions.

The Bottom of a Partition

![](../../../assets/e2d96fe78dd2.jpg)

The bottom edge of each partition has the following:

1. A bracket screwed on that sticks out a bit.  This bracket will snap onto the nubs of the adjoining partition.  There is also an adjustable round leg (or foot) that can be screwed up or down to set the partition properly on the floor.
2. Two silver colored nubs on the far side from the bracket. These will snap onto the adjoining partition’s bracket. This side also has the adjustable round leg.

## Appendix

Connecting partitions with T-foot

A T-foot is added to provide stability to a wall of partitions and is used on the side walls, to replace a T-partition, so as not to block the AT’s view of the students (and the students’ view of the AT). 

A T-foot replaces a bracket at the bottom of the partition. The round adjusting leg is also removed from the end of the partition that has the T-foot. As the bracket is removed, the adjoining partition’s nubs cannot snap into anything. So, to stabilize the connection, the adjoining partition also must have a T-foot and the two T-feet are held together with a long plastic tie.  If the plastic tie is not long enough, connect two of them back-to-back. Wrap the tie around both T-feet and then securely tighten the tie. 

The top of the partitions are bolted on with Allen bolts just like any other. 

For futher stability, tape them together onto the floor as below, with duck tape
